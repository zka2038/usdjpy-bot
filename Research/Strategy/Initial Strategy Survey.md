# Four USDJPY signal families die out-of-sample

The honest answer to "what systematic strategies are candidates for USDJPY at a 1–10 trading day holding period" is that **four of the five channels usually proposed are either dead out-of-sample or contemporaneous rather than predictive, and the published evidence supports no standalone directional edge at this horizon**. Daily-to-25-day technical/trend rules on G11 currencies produce out-of-sample net Sharpe ratios of **−0.08 to +0.19, none statistically significant**, after averaging **0.62 in-sample** across the same 388 portfolios ([RIBAF 2022](https://pureadmin.qub.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/370624058/1_s2.0_S0275531922001659_main_1_.pdf)). Every quantified calendar effect in USDJPY — Gotobi, the Tokyo 09:55 fix, the London 16:00 fix — is an **intraday** effect of 1.8bp to 5 pips that requires a minutes-long hold and has visibly decayed. The risk-appetite channel's cleanest test finds a next-week return coefficient on signed ΔVIX of **−0.03 (SE 0.11), R² ≈ 0.00** — statistically indistinguishable from nothing ([NBER WP 14473](https://www.nber.org/papers/w14473.pdf)). And carry is arithmetically irrelevant here: a ~2.6pp policy differential earns roughly **10bp gross over ten trading days** against a 10-day realised volatility of 170–190bp. What survives is narrower and stranger than the standard retail menu: **a dated-flow hypothesis (Japanese fiscal/turn-of-month), an official-intervention reaction hypothesis built on MOF rhetoric and the BOJ's 18:00 JST disclosure, and a rate-path-revision hypothesis that trades changes and residuals rather than levels**. Several decision-critical quantities — USDJPY daily autocorrelations, positioning-conditional return tables, any sound daily ML study — simply do not exist in the published record and must be computed in-house. Two timestamp traps in the obvious free data manufacture apparent predictability out of nothing, and both must be fixed before any number is believed.

## Trend, calendar, carry and risk appetite each fail for a different reason

The four negative results are not one result repeated; they fail through four distinct mechanisms, which matters because it closes four different escape routes.

**Trend died empirically, and the decay is dated.** The single most decision-relevant study has exactly the right design — G11 currencies, daily data 1980–April 2020, holding periods of **1, 5, 10 and 25 days**, 388 rule portfolios across filter, moving-average, channel-breakout and trading-range families, a true 1980–1998 / 1999–2020 split, and net-of-cost reporting. Out-of-sample mean return across the grid falls from **3.29% to 0.28%**, and restricting to the 255 variants that worked in-sample still gives a net out-of-sample mean of **−0.07%** and Sharpe of **−0.01**; the in/out Sharpe difference is **−0.56 with t = −37.44** ([RIBAF 2022](https://pureadmin.qub.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/370624058/1_s2.0_S0275531922001659_main_1_.pdf)). A futures time-series-momentum study with an explicit daily grid finds the **(1-day, 1-day) strategy at Sharpe 2.63 before 1994 and 0.37 after** ([Baltas & Kosowski](https://www.naaim.org/wp-content/uploads/2013/10/00S_Momentum_Strategies_in_Futures_Markets_Nick_Baltas.pdf)), and a two-century study finds "no sign of a statistical degradation of long trends, whereas shorter trends have significantly withered," with a **3-day trend effect largely gone since about 2003** ([Lempérière et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1404.3274)). The JPY-specific version is sharper still: the Levich-Thomas 1% filter on the yen went from **12.48% / Sharpe 1.16 (1976–1990) to 0.49% / Sharpe 0.04 (1991–2005)**, and in Dueker-Neely Markov rules **JPY showed the largest decline (~10.5 points) and was the only currency where equal means was rejected** ([Neely, Weller & Ulrich](https://files.stlouisfed.org/files/htdocs/wp/2006/2006-046.pdf)). The classic USDJPY MA(1,150) result — **8.35% p.a., t = 2.85, Sharpe 0.76, 1979–1992, gross** — collapses to **3.53% with t = 1.21** once official intervention days are excluded, and **7.84 of those 8.35 points accrued overnight rather than in the 9 AM-to-close session** ([FRB St. Louis WP 2011-001](https://files.stlouisfed.org/files/htdocs/wp/2011/2011-001.pdf)). That rule also trades about **5 times a year**, so it is not a 1–10 day strategy in the first place. Cost is not the binding objection — at a modern 2bp round-trip spread a 10-day holding needs only ~50bp/yr of gross edge to break even, an inference from the researcher's own arithmetic — the binding objection is that measured gross edge is also near zero.

**Calendar effects are real as flow and intraday as price.** The Gotobi settlement mechanism is documented in peer-reviewed work: the 09:55 JST dummy in a USDJPY spike regression carries a coefficient of **1.08 (t = 42.5)**, versus **−1.01 (t = −27.8)** for the London 15:00 GMT fix, so in this pair Tokyo, not London, is the dominant fixing event. But the return is **about 1.8bp over fifteen years on a 15-minute round trip**, with no reported t-statistic, and the pricing behaviour that generated it has decayed — BTMU's fixing max-gap averaged **+2.47bp before November 2008 and −0.79bp after**, and spike frequency at 00:55 GMT fell from **0.643 in 2008 to 0.121 in 2013** ([NBER WP 22820](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w22820/w22820.pdf)). Note the ordering in the same data: **month-end 0.538 > Friday 0.301 > 5th/10th 0.276 > all days 0.247** — a Gotobi-only screen is partly picking up Friday and month-end. For a days-to-week strategy these effects are **execution-timing controls, not signals**: avoid initiating or liquidating in the 09:50–10:00 JST window on 5th/10th days, Fridays and month-end, and in the 15:55–16:05 London window, where abnormal volatility persisted after the February 2015 reform and realised execution cost rose by about **1 basis point** ([NBER WP 23327](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w23327/w23327.pdf)).

**Carry cannot carry the strategy.** With the Fed at **3.75–4.00%** ([Federal Reserve, 28 Oct 2026](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20261028a.htm)) and the BOJ at **around 1.25%** ([BOJ, 18 Sep 2026](https://www.boj.or.jp/en/mopo/mpmdeci/mpr_2026/k260918a.pdf)), the differential is ≈2.6pp — the researcher's arithmetic puts fair carry at **≈$7.19 per night per standard lot**, ≈10bp gross over ten trading days and ≈6bp after the observed retail skim, against 10-day volatility of 170–190bp, a carry/risk ratio of **0.03–0.06**. The skim itself is large: across a cross-broker snapshot the median long swap captures roughly **60% of fair carry while the median short pays about 1.8x fair**, a two-sided ≈**1.4–2.0pp annualised per side** ([EarnForex](https://www.earnforex.com/forex-swaps/)) — all of that conversion is the researcher's own arithmetic from cited rate levels, not a sourced figure. Worse, the pooled academic evidence says a wide differential predicts **more negative skewness (coefficient −23.92 at t+1 quarter)**, so "long USDJPY when carry is wide" deliberately loads the state where crash risk is highest, and JPY is the extreme currency on both skewness (**+0.318**) and risk reversal (**1.059**) in that sample ([NBER WP 14473](https://www.nber.org/papers/w14473.pdf)).

**Risk appetite is contemporaneous.** The yen's equity beta is **insignificant in every regime** of the best-identified threshold study (**−0.029 full sample**, monthly, effective rate), while the Swiss franc's is **−0.086\*\*\***; what is significant for JPY is the interest differential (**−7.499\*\*\***) and its interaction with volatility (**+0.243\*\*\***), i.e. the yen is a funding currency whose risk-off strength is intermediated by carry unwind, not a hedge currency ([Bundesbank DP 34/2014](https://bundesbank.de/resource/blob/703880/7b75b244043eedf39cc3063293f01bf7/mL/2014-11-24-dkp-34-data.pdf)). The weekly test is decisive: signed ΔVIX moves same-week carry returns (**−0.43, SE 0.11**) and predicts **next-week positioning (−1.29, SE 0.57)** but not next-week returns (**−0.03, SE 0.11**) ([NBER WP 14473](https://www.nber.org/papers/w14473.pdf)). Positions unwind after the price has moved. August 2024 is the illustration — trigger on the **2 August payrolls** print, epicentre on **5 August**, and markets "stabilised quickly" ([BIS Bulletin 90](https://www.bis.org/publ/bisbull90.pdf); [BIS QR Sept 2024](https://www.bis.org/publ/qtrpdf/r_qt2409a.pdf)) — a signal reacting to the realised VIX spike enters after the move. One conditioning idea does survive, and it is the researcher's inference rather than a published strategy: condition the risk signal on the **sign of the concurrent US yield move**, because the yen's safe-haven failures cluster in inflationary sell-offs (1973–74, 1979, 1980–82, 2000–02, 2022) where rates and risk channels fight ([Man Group](https://www.man.com/insights/road-ahead-yen-behaving-badly)).

## Three hypotheses have a dated counterparty and the right horizon

What remains are mechanisms with an identifiable party that must trade on a known date, which is the only category where the horizon matches and the evidence is not already arbitraged. None of the three has a published backtest; all three are hypotheses to be tested, stated with what would falsify them.

| Hypothesis | Core claim to test | Required series |
|---|---|---|
| **H1 Intervention reaction** | Conditional on spot within ~100 pips of 160, escalated MOF rhetoric, and a BOJ 18:00 JST settlement-estimate release, 1–10 day USDJPY returns are negative | USDJPY bid/ask daily + H1; MOF FEIO monthly/quarterly; BOJ daily money-market settlement estimates; wire-timestamped rhetoric |
| **H2 Japanese dated flow** | Mean USDJPY return in the last 10 business days of March, the first 5 of April, and turn-of-month windows differs from unconditional | 20–30y daily USDJPY; MOF weekly/monthly Transactions in Securities; ITA Japan monthly toshin net foreign purchases |
| **H3 Rate-path revision** | The n-day *change* in (US 2y − JGB 2y), correctly timestamped, or the residual from a contemporaneous fair-value fit, predicts 1–10 day returns | FRED DGS2/DGS10 or Treasury CSV; MOF `jgbcme_all.csv`; CME SOFR futures strip |

**H1 is the best-evidenced and the most dangerous to trade.** Ten confirmed yen-buying operation days since 2022 across six clusters, with 2026 alone totalling **¥27,134.2bn** — more than double 2022 and 2024 combined — and the **30 July–3 August 2026 cluster at ¥15,399.3bn a monthly record, with the US Treasury joining on 31 July for the first time since 1998** ([MOF 2026 3Q](https://www.mof.go.jp/english/policy/international_policy/reference/feio/quarter/2026_3Qe.html); [japan.co.jp](https://japan.co.jp/e/reports/japan-record-yen-intervention-15-trillion-2026.html)). The formally estimated modern reaction function is **trend-deviation-driven, not velocity-driven**: over 2004/8–2018/3 the rate-of-change term is insignificant while deviation from the five-year moving average is significant (ordered probit **−33.32, t ≈ −2.9**) ([Ito & Yabu](https://www.fbc.keio.ac.jp/~tyabu/JapaneseForexInterventions.pdf)) — which is the statistical analogue of "defend 160" and contradicts officials' own "speed not levels" language. Rhetoric is the one zero-lag input: the final rung ("the time for decisive action is approaching," 30 April 2026) led the operation by **0 days**, and the "decisive measures" rung by **2–6 days**, squarely inside the holding window. The statistically strongest trade in the whole dataset is not the operation but the **disclosure**: the BOJ's 18:00 JST settlement-figure release moves USDJPY **−0.172 to −0.269 yen (t ≈ −3.1 to −5.0)**, peaking near **−0.463 yen / −0.246% around midnight JST**, with placebo, randomisation and synthetic-control robustness checks, and **next-day realised volatility falls significantly (GARCH dummy −1.070 to −1.145)** ([Lu, Urushidani & Kameda](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1059056026004247)). Against it: **n = 10**, with only **7 of 10** event days and **6 of 10** fifth-observations in the yen's favour ([Grizzly Parrot](https://grizzlyparrottrading.com/futures-basics/6j-boj-intervention-signals.html)) — the standard error on a 60% hit rate at n=10 is about 15 percentage points, so this cannot be established from the event history alone. Retracement is typically **60–80% over 4–6 weeks**, and the one episode that did not retrace (July 2024) coincided with a BOJ hike, so persistence is conditional on a monetary driver — both figures come from a single broker piece with documented internal numerical contradictions and should be treated as low-reliability. Execution is the real constraint: **160.17 → 156.60 in about 11 minutes** on 29 April 2024, full-day ranges of **516 and 575 pips** in 2026, and a **rebound to 160.875** on 31 July that would have swept shorts too. MOF has repeatedly chosen thin windows — Japanese public holidays on 4 and 6 May 2026, a Friday US-hours joint operation on 31 July. A hard stop is not protection here; size for a 500-pip gap or buy a 1-week put instead.

**H2 has the right horizon and essentially no evidence, which is precisely why it is worth testing yourself.** The mechanism is statutory: Japanese corporates and insurers close books on 31 March and 30 September, insurers publish FY investment plans in April and revise around October. Rigorous support exists only indirectly and only for the *month-end* variant — relative spike frequency **0.538 at month-end vs 0.247 on all days**, and an end-of-month fix reversal strategy that was profitable pre-reform **including USDJPY** and strengthened post-reform at the 15-minute horizon (EUR/USD **0.617 → 1.21bp**) while intra-month trading was unprofitable in both periods ([NBER WP 22820](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w22820/w22820.pdf); [NBER WP 23327](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w23327/w23327.pdf)). There is also a genuinely new flow absent from every academic sample: **foreign-equity fund net inflows averaged ¥1.7tn per month from June to August 2026, the fastest since at least 2010, with ¥15.4tn in the twelve months to August 2026**, against households' foreign assets rising from ¥58tn (March 2010) to **¥189tn (June 2026)** ([Takuji Okubo](https://takujiokubo.substack.com/p/mrs-watanabe-no-more)). Against it: the only USDJPY monthly return table found (**March −0.8% / April +1.1%**) states **no sample period, no sample size and no significance tests**, and contradicts a half-year-end story with August −0.9% / September +0.6% ([SeasOptima](https://www.seasoptima.com/en/blog/usdjpy-seasonal-patterns)) — unusable. A minimal calendar grid on one pair is **50–60 near-independent tests**, so 2–3 nominally significant effects are expected by chance, and an annual effect gives one observation a year: 25 years is 25 observations, so even a genuine 50-pip effect with a 150-pip standard deviation yields **t ≈ 1.7**. Absence of significance would not be evidence of absence; a significant result would not be trustworthy without a correction for the size of the search. Note too that GPIF is **not** a calendar effect — rebalancing is band-triggered with ±5–6% tolerances and no fixed frequency, and all four buckets sat within ~2pp of target at March 2026 ([GPIF Annual Report FY2025](https://www.gpif.go.jp/en/topics/annual_report_fiscal_year_2025.pdf)). Treat it as a control variable.

**H3 trades the derivative, not the level.** In high-correlation regimes the differential is almost entirely a US-rates signal: on a one-month window the US 2y and 10y swap rates each correlated **~96%** with USDJPY while the **Japan 10y swap correlated −14%** ([MNI](https://www.mnimarkets.com/articles/us-rate-moves-still-dominating-correlations-with-jpy)). But the relationship is unstable: a 12-week rolling correlation of USDJPY returns against the US–JP 10y spread **averaged +0.43 before October 2025, peaked at 0.91, then averaged −0.07 with eight consecutive negative weeks** ([MUFG via FXStreet](https://www.fxstreet.com/news/usd-jpy-decouples-from-yield-spreads-mufg-202512051021)). All of that is contemporaneous and therefore not tradeable as stated; the predictive evidence sits at **multi-quarter** horizons (pooled differential coefficient **2.17 at t+1 quarter**, decaying to **−0.04 by t+10**) ([NBER WP 14473](https://www.nber.org/papers/w14473.pdf)). Two forms could still work and neither is ruled out: **under-reaction drift** on the n-day *change* in the spread, and **mean reversion of the residual** from a contemporaneous fair-value fit — the kind of residual MUFG's and Macrobond's models exist to produce. One dated conditional statistic points the right way, with the caveat that its **sample size and period are not stated**: when US–Japan 2-year spreads compressed by **≥15bp over five sessions, USDJPY fell in every instance, averaging −3.92%** ([Investing.com, 23 Nov 2026](https://www.investing.com/analysis/usdjpy-weekly-outlook-yen-carry-unwind-risks-flash-red-200692089)). The regime argues for revisions over levels: as priced, the December 2026 Fed and BOJ hikes leave the differential **essentially unchanged near 262bp** (the researcher's arithmetic), which is why USDJPY sits near 160 rather than rallying on BOJ hikes.

Positioning belongs in all three as a state variable, not a signal. The documented extremes are **−184,223 contracts net short JPY on 2 July 2024** and **−163,412 on 28 July 2026** ([l0g.fr](https://l0g.fr/en/analysis/yen-163412-net-short-contracts-before-shock/)), and in both cases the extreme print came **1–5 weeks** before the violent appreciation, not days. The configuration as of mid-November 2026 inverts the 2024 setup: **+123.1K contracts net long JPY, 94th percentile, the most crowded exposure in the data** ([FXStreet, 17 Nov 2026](https://www.fxstreet.com/analysis/cftc-report-oil-and-gold-weaken-as-yen-exposure-reaches-an-extreme-202611171255)), so a contrarian-at-extremes rule is now USDJPY-*bullish* and in direct conflict with the intervention overlay that caps 160. A systematic design must resolve that conflict explicitly rather than average the two. **Source conflict to carry forward, not resolve:** dated prints for the weeks ending 10 and 17 November 2026 both report JPY net long ([Newsquawk](https://www.newsquawk.com/headlines/cftc-positioning-we-17th-nov-usd-net-long-eur-net-short-jpy-net-long-gbp-net-short-aud-net-short-nzd-net-short-cad-net-short-chf-net-short)), while an undated 2026 print reports a **92.2K net short** ([VT Markets](https://www.vtmarkets.com/en-ca/live-updates/cftc-data-show-yen-shorts-deepen-as-speculators-lift-net-short-positions-to-92-2k-contracts/)); these cannot be reconciled without the dated series, and the dated prints should be preferred.

## Two timestamp traps manufacture predictability from nothing

These are the most important engineering points in the entire project, and both produce *positive* results from no signal.

**Trap one: FRED's `DEXJPUS` is a 12:00 ET noon buying-rate snapshot, not a 16:00 ET close.** Pairing it with a 16:00 ET equity or VIX close means the "predictor" is observed four hours *after* the "predicted" FX print. That mechanically converts part of a contemporaneous relationship into an apparent predictive one — the single most common source of false positives in exactly this research question. The Nikkei version is worse: the Tokyo close precedes the US close by roughly 14 hours, so "yesterday's Nikkei predicting USDJPY" is often same-information-set arithmetic.

**Trap two: a matched-date US-minus-Japan yield spread mixes two information timestamps across the date line.** Treasury's par yield curve for date *T* rests on late-afternoon US quotes and is published after the US close on *T* — so a *T*-stamped US yield was not knowable during the Tokyo session of *T*. MOF publishes the JGB curve for Japanese business day *T* in the evening JST, which is *before* the US session of the same calendar date. The naive `DGS2 − JGB2y` on matching dates is therefore **not a tradeable signal**. The fix is to fix one daily decision timestamp (e.g. 00:00 UTC) and lag each leg to the last value genuinely published before it — a one-day lag for the US leg, none for the JGB leg. Japanese and US holiday calendars also differ, so the spread is one-sidedly stale on Golden Week and similar stretches.

Three further alignment errors in the same family. **COT is a Tuesday snapshot released Friday ~15:30 ET**, so the earliest actionable bar is the following Monday open ([CFTC](https://www.cftc.gov/MarketReports/CommitmentsofTraders/AbouttheCOTReports/cot_about.html)) — aligning a COT signal to its Tuesday reference date embeds up to four trading days of look-ahead, which in JPY is catastrophic because the 2024 unwind and the 2026 intervention days fall inside exactly that window. **MOF publishes daily intervention amounts only quarterly** — the July–September 2026 daily detail appeared on **9 November 2026**, roughly 100 days later — so intervention amounts can never be a contemporaneous input; only price action and the BOJ's 18:00 JST estimate are inside the horizon. And **HistData's M1 bars are built from the bid tick only**, with ask available solely in the Generic ASCII tick format, and timestamps in **Eastern Standard Time with no daylight-saving adjustment** ([HistData FAQ](https://www.histdata.com/f-a-q/)) — bid-only bars are optimistic for longs and pessimistic for shorts, asymmetrically, and will not wash out. FirstRate uses DST-observing US Eastern ([FirstRate Data](https://firstratedata.com/)), so the two cannot be concatenated without re-stamping.

## The gaps are large, named, and cheap to close in-house

Several quantities this question needs do not exist in the accessible literature. They should be treated as experiments, not as holes to be filled with inference.

**No USDJPY daily return autocorrelations at lags 1–10 were found in any source**, and no Hurst exponent or variance ratio either — the most promising source, a thesis estimating the Hurst exponent via detrending moving-average analysis on ~40 years of daily closes for 17 pairs including USD/JPY, shows **only table captions in its retrievable text, with its one numeric parameter table covering CHF/JPY rather than USD/JPY** ([Boeker, UCL](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10137352/13/Maria%20Elisabeth%20Boeker%20PhD%20Thesis.pdf)). Do not impute a value. This is a one-afternoon computation from free data and is the first thing to run. **No study regresses USDJPY 1–10 day returns on JPY COT positioning percentile or z-score** — no coefficients, no t-statistics, no hit rates, no conditional-return tables; every source treats positioning as crowding rather than a tested predictor. **No methodologically sound out-of-sample daily USDJPY ML study exists.** The credible FX ML evidence is monthly and pooled, and its yen result is the headline for this project: with 70 predictors and ~300 months, **Japan's out-of-sample R² is 0.19–0.28% and insignificant**, against a composite of 1.51% (elastic net) to 1.86% (ensemble), while **OLS on the same predictors returns −8.74% composite and −12.36% for Japan** ([Filippou, Rapach, Taylor & Zhou](https://jfqa.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/26402_Econ_Fundamentals_Machine_Learning.pdf)). The one daily transformer paper covering USD/JPY reports an **MSFE ratio of 54.3–61.1 against a random walk**, implying an out-of-sample R² near 46% alongside a Sharpe of 1.891 — two claims that cannot describe the same tradable signal, with unlagged contemporaneous cross-asset covariates and a holding window selected on the test period as the likely causes ([arXiv:2512.12727](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.12727v1)). Also unsourced: any carry-versus-spot decomposition of FX trend returns; any BOJ-regime-conditioned trend study; any published "yen per 100bp" beta; session-by-session USDJPY spread figures; actual 2026 broker swap tables; USDJPY slippage during intervention; and FX-specific Sharpe figures for volatility targeting.

The data stack to close these is free. **A $0 stack is genuinely sufficient** for an honest days-to-week study: OANDA v20 candles with **`price=BA`** for real bid *and* ask OHLC from a dealable book (the parameter **defaults to `M`**, which is the silent trap — [OANDA v20](https://developer.oanda.com/rest-live-v20/instrument-ep/)); **Databento's $125 new-user credit** against CME GLBX.MDP3 (16+ years, TBBO/OHLCV plus the `Definition` and `Statistics` schemas that make a defensible continuous series possible at all — [Databento](https://databento.com/datasets/GLBX.MDP3)); **FRED DGS2 from 1976-06-01 and DGS10 from 1962-01-02** ([FRED](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/categories/115)) or Treasury's CSV; **MOF `jgbcme_all.csv` from 1974** as a single direct download ([MOF](https://www.mof.go.jp/english/policy/jgbs/reference/interest_rate/index.htm)); **CFTC bulk COT zips**, where the trade-off must be stated rather than resolved — legacy futures-only runs from **1986** but the informative Dealer/Asset Manager/Leveraged Funds breakdown (TFF) only from **September 2009**, with a "Starting July 20, 2010" note implying a possible definitional break ([CFTC](https://www.cftc.gov/MarketReports/CommitmentsofTraders/HistoricalCompressed/index.htm)); and an event calendar built from official agency release schedules. That last choice costs you the forecast column, and therefore surprise magnitudes — but at this horizon an event-*aware* backtest mostly needs release timestamps, not surprise sizes, and the scraped forecast column is someone else's licensed consensus and the single largest licensing exposure if any of this is published. If CME futures are the deployment venue, note the inversion trap: **6J and MJY are quoted as USD per JPY, the inverse of spot**, with MJY at **1,250,000 JPY notional, $1.25 tick value and ~$180 average Micro G5 initial margin** ([CME](https://www.cmegroup.com/markets/microsuite/fx.html)) — specs that could not be verified against CME's own contract-spec page and must be confirmed before sizing. Several vendor pages are internally inconsistent and were flagged rather than reconciled: EODHD's comparison table claims 30+ years of intraday while its own FAQ says 1-minute from 2004 and other intervals from October 2020 ([EODHD](https://eodhd.com/pricing)); Polygon.io's pricing now redirects to Massive, where **no forex tier is listed at all**; TradingEconomics' Japan 2-year page contradicts itself on levels and historical highs; and a zero-markup-claiming broker's swap page labels the same number both a cost and a positive carry ([Afterprime](https://afterprime.com/swaps/usdjpy)).

## The bar a result must clear, and what to expect after clearing it

Set the bar before searching. The battery that the overfitting literature actually supports is four simultaneous gates: a **Newey–West t-statistic above 3.0** with lag truncation at least the holding period — the figure derived from 316 catalogued factors, where the Bonferroni benchmark reaches **3.78 by 2012** and the authors take **3.18** as a minimum after adjusting for unobserved factors ([Harvey, Liu & Zhu](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20592.pdf)); a **Deflated Sharpe Ratio above 0.95** with N estimated by clustering correlated trials rather than counting raw backtests ([quantt.co.uk](https://www.quantt.co.uk/resources/deflated-sharpe-ratio)); a **Probability of Backtest Overfitting below 0.05** via CSCV with **S = 16 (12,780 combinations)** ([Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado & Zhu](https://escholarship.org/content/qt4w1110bb/qt4w1110bb.pdf)); and a block-bootstrap interval with **20–40 day blocks** excluding zero. Validation is CPCV with purging equal to the maximum holding period plus one bar on both sides and a forward-only embargo, reporting the **distribution** of path Sharpes and the fraction negative, not the mean ([purged cross-validation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purged_cross-validation)). Log the full return vector of every configuration from day one — PBO cannot be retrofitted. The calibration device that matters: a **pure random walk over 1,000 daily prices and 8,800 parameter combinations produced an in-sample Sharpe of 1.27 with a PBO of 55%**, and in one worked example all in-sample Sharpes were positive while **about 78% of out-of-sample Sharpes were negative** ([Bailey et al.](https://escholarship.org/content/qt4w1110bb/qt4w1110bb.pdf)). An in-sample Sharpe of 1 is the null expectation here, not evidence. The effective sample is also far smaller than the bar count: at a 5-day hold, 20 years of daily data is roughly **1,040 non-overlapping episodes**, not 5,200 — so a t of 3.0 requires an annualised Sharpe near **0.67 over 20 years, 1.0 over 9 years, 1.5 over 4 years** (the researcher's arithmetic). Note one unresolved conflict in the cost literature worth not papering over: the key G11 study states cost schedules of **0.18% / 0.06% / 0.03%** in one section and **6bp then 3bp** in another, and never reconciles them; the basis-point figures match the market-spread evidence, so the percentage figures look like a units error ([RIBAF 2022](https://pureadmin.qub.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/370624058/1_s2.0_S0275531922001659_main_1_.pdf)).

Then expect disappointment, and plan for it. Costs are first-order: raw-spread accounts show USDJPY at **0.0–0.5 spread plus $2.50–$3.50 per lot per side** and spread-only accounts at **1.0–1.5** ([BrokerChooser](https://brokerchooser.com/best-brokers/lowest-usdjpy-spread-metatrader5-brokers-in-japan)), which converts to roughly **1.0–2.8 pips per round trip** and a realistic central assumption of ~1.5 pips, or about **1.0% of notional a year** at two round trips a week — the conversion arithmetic is the researcher's. The **Wednesday triple swap** is not a detail: a position held past 17:00 ET on Wednesday is charged three calendar days ([Pepperstone](https://pepperstone.com/en-gb/learn-to-trade/trading-guides/what-are-swaps-how-to-calculate-swaps/)), so with a five-day average hold essentially every trade pays or receives seven calendar days of carry over five trading days — a 40% distortion whose sign depends on position direction and therefore does not cancel. Stops must carry a one-sided adverse cost, and the two published execution datasets disagree by a wide margin — Saxo reports **9.3% of USDJPY resting stops negatively slipped, averaging 0.2 pips** ([Saxo](https://home.saxo/en-gb/rates-and-conditions/forex/orders-and-execution)) while FXCM reports **57.42% of stop orders negatively slipped** across all instruments ([FXCM](https://www.fxcm.com/ca/execution/slippage-statistics/)); model the pessimistic end. Finally, apply the degradation haircut: across **~215 bank alternative-beta indices from 17 sponsors**, the **median Sharpe deterioration from backtest to live was 73%**, with at least a 50% haircut in every asset class, **only 18 of 215 (8.4%)** matching their backtest, **65 of 215 (~30%)** going negative live, and the most complex strategies haircut **more than 30 percentage points harder** than the simplest ([Suhonen, Lennkh & Perez via Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/?p=36892)). A backtest Sharpe of 1.0 implies roughly 0.3 live; 0.6 implies about 0.2, which after retail costs is indistinguishable from zero.

Order of work, given all of the above: **run the autocorrelation and variance-ratio computation first** (it is free, it is the single largest named gap, and a lag-1 to lag-10 structure indistinguishable from zero would correctly kill the entire mean-reversion family before any parameter is fitted); then **test H1 as an event study** rather than a strategy, because its statistically supported component — the 18:00 JST disclosure drift with t ≈ 3–5 — is a 0–1 day overlay and should be validated on its own terms before anything is layered on it; then **H2**, with the multiple-testing correction specified in advance and the result reported honestly whichever way it falls. Keep model complexity to **3–10 effective parameters**: the researcher's k/T reasoning says each free parameter costs about 1/2500 of explained variance against a total available R² that may only be 0.1–0.5%, and the independent evidence agrees — equal-weighted volatility-scaled combination of a few pre-specified signals is the design the literature supports, since a simple average of two models beat both components on R² and **portfolio optimisation needs ~3,000 months of data to beat 1/N with 25 assets** ([DeMiguel, Garlappi & Uppal](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v22y2009i5p1915-1953.html)). Volatility targeting will not help the Sharpe — its effect in currencies is **"negligible"**, material only in equities where the leverage effect exists ([Harvey et al.](https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Published_Papers/P135_The_impact_of.pdf)) — though it does stabilise realised volatility and trim the left tail, which is reason enough to use it for risk control.

## Conclusion

The most useful thing this body of evidence offers is not a strategy but a correctly specified null. The decay pattern is remarkably consistent across every channel examined, and it is dated: short trend broke in the mid-1990s to early 2000s, the Tokyo fix's pricing edge broke after November 2008, post-reform London order-flow predictability had a half-life of **about three months in USDJPY**, and the yen is repeatedly the *worst* major in the cross-section — the weak case in the credible ML study, one of only two currencies where a regime model failed even on direction, and the pair where Markov trading rules decayed most sharply. A researcher who sets out expecting to find a 1–10 day USDJPY edge and finds none has reproduced the literature correctly. That is a publishable result and a far better outcome than a Sharpe of 1.2 that turns out to be a bid-only price series paired with a noon FX snapshot.

What this reframes is where to look. The surviving hypotheses share a property the dead ones lack: a named counterparty who must transact on a known date for a non-speculative reason — the MOF defending a level, Japanese insurers closing books, retail fund installments settling. That is also why they are under-researched: they are institutionally specific, they resist pooling across currencies, and the toshin flow in particular — **¥1.7tn a month of largely unhedged foreign-equity buying, a standing yen-negative bid** — did not exist in the samples underpinning any of the papers above. The realistic best case is a small, event-conditioned overlay with a net Sharpe of 0–0.4 and wide confidence intervals, deployed in size small enough that a 500-pip intervention gap is survivable, and genuinely valuable mostly as a finished piece of research. Judge the project by whether the harness is honest enough to produce a credible negative, because that is the most likely outcome and the one the evidence predicts.
