# Test Gotobi and trend first, skip breakouts

Of the USDJPY strategies a retail trader can run on Interactive Brokers, two have both documented evidence and a real chance of beating costs. The first is the **Tokyo 9:55 JST fix / gotobi flow effect**: a USD-buying drift into the fix, followed by a partial reversal. The second is a **low-turnover, volatility-scaled trend-plus-carry position** with crash-risk filters. Neither is a sure thing. The Tokyo-fix edge is a few basis points a day. It lost money at full quoted spreads in the best academic study, it decayed sharply after 2014, and nobody has published a test for 2021–2026. Trend and carry have strong multi-asset evidence, but on a single pair they are noisy, and in 2024 and 2026 they were hit by 4–8 yen intervention and unwind gaps. Most of the rest of the usual retail toolkit has no support after costs in the evidence reviewed. That includes London 4pm fix fades, Asian-range or London-open breakouts, RSI-style mean reversion, news scalping around NFP and the BoJ, CME 6J order-flow tools, MOF weekly flow data, and March repatriation seasonality. Some of these are useful as risk filters or event calendars, but not as entry signals. The base rate is sobering: **74–89% of retail CFD/FX accounts lose money** across EU regulators, and 59.6% do so even at IBKR. So the right first step is to rebuild the two most specific effects on 2021–2026 data with your own measured IBKR spreads, before writing any execution code.

## Costs on IdealPro set the bar every edge must clear

The rest of this report hinges on one number: what a round trip actually costs. IBKR charges **0.20bp of trade value with a $2 minimum per order** on spot FX at Tier I, which covers up to $1bn a month. Spreads come from 17 dealers with no markup, quoted "as narrow as 1/10 PIP" ([IBKR](https://www.interactivebrokers.com/en/pricing/commissions-spot-currencies.php?region=americas)). IdealPro accepts orders of $25,000 to $7m. Smaller orders go to the odd-lot venue, where limit prices are not displayed and fills "may take longer" ([IBKR](https://www.interactivebrokers.com/en/trading/newFXProMinMax.php); [IBKR FAQ](https://www.interactivebrokers.com/support/faq/content/idealpro-minimum-mean-65996956)). Orders that persist overnight count as new orders for the minimum ([IBKR](https://www.interactivebrokers.com/en/pricing/commissions-spot-currencies.php?region=americas)).

The cost per trade works out as follows. On a $100k round trip, commission is 2 × $2 = $4, or **0.4bp**. At USDJPY ~155, a 0.3–0.5 pip spread adds about 0.2–0.3bp. That gives roughly **0.6–0.8bp all-in during liquid hours**, and several times that around NFP, CPI, BoJ decisions and the 17:00 New York rollover. The spread part is an estimate: IBKR does not publish USDJPY spreads by session, so you need to log them yourself through the API. At $25k orders the $2 minimum alone costs 0.8bp per side, which rules out scalping at small size.

Futures do not get around this. A CME 6J contract is ¥12.5m with a $6.25 tick, about 0.7bp. The relaunched Micro JPY (MJY) is ¥1.25m with a $1.25 tick, about **1.4bp per tick**, which is proportionally coarser ([CME rulebook](https://cmegroup.com/content/dam/cmegroup/rulebook/CME/III/250/253/253.pdf); [CME MJY specs](https://cmegroup.com/markets/fx/g10/micro-jpy-usd_contract_specifications.html)). Both are quoted JPY/USD, the inverse of spot ([StoneX](https://futures.stonex.com/blog/understanding-japanese-yen-futures)). Leverage is capped at **30:1 on majors** for UK/EU retail CFDs and rolling spot ([ESMA](https://www.esma.europa.eu/pl/node/84933)) and at **25:1 for Japanese retail FX margin** since 2011 ([Risk.net](https://www.risk.net/foreign-exchange/2099718/japans-new-leverage-limit-will-hurt-carry-trade-says-glenn-stevens)). Whether IBKR applies a 30:1-equivalent margin to a UK client's spot FX (as opposed to CFDs) was not confirmed, so check that directly.

Leverage constraints matter for more than compliance. Heimer's natural experiment around the 2010 US 50:1 cap found that the cap "reduces average per-trade losses even after adjusting for risk". Per-trade ROI improved by about 0.10–0.15pp, and the average retail trade lost about 0.2% ([Cleveland Fed WP 14-33](https://www.clevelandfed.org/-/media/project/clevelandfedtenant/clevelandfedsite/publications/working-papers/2014/wp-1433-can-leverage-constraints-help-investors-pdf.pdf)). Against a cost bar of 0.6–0.8bp, a strategy that targets 1–3bp a day has no room for error, while one that trades monthly barely notices costs. That asymmetry drives the ranking below.

## Flow effects are real, small, and decaying

### The Tokyo fix and gotobi days are the best-documented intraday edge

Japanese importers settle at the bank fixing rate set at **9:55 JST (00:55 GMT, all year, because Japan has no DST)**. Ito and Yamada show that customer orders at the Tokyo fix are biased toward buying foreign currency, and that the bias is predictable. The yen weakens into the fix and the price path reverses afterward. The effect is strongest on the 5th and 10th of the month and at month-end ([NBER w22820](https://www.nber.org/papers/w22820)). Their simplest trade was long USDJPY for the 5 minutes before 00:55 GMT and short for the 5 minutes after. It earned **about 1.8bp a day over 1999–2013**, which the authors describe as only "slightly above" bid-ask costs. One-minute spike frequency at the fix more than doubles at month-end, from 0.247 on all days to **0.538** ([NBER PDF](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w22820/w22820.pdf)).

Krohn, Mueller and Whelan (Journal of Finance, 2024) put this in a broader framework, using data for nine currencies over 1999–2019. USDJPY shows the **largest post-Tokyo-fix reversal** of all of them: +7.94% annualised (t = 8.22), against −4.06% before the fix. The economics are blunt. The yen reversal strategy returned about 12.0% a year with no costs, **about 2.7% paying half the quoted spread, and about −6.5% paying the full spread**. The dollar-portfolio post-Tokyo reversal held at 6–7% a year through 2014, then **fell to 2.08% in 2014–2019** ([BoC SWP 2021-48](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/swp2021-48.pdf); [WRAP/JF](https://wrap.warwick.ac.uk/id/eprint/177333)).

The most tradeable-looking result comes from Bessho, Sugimoto and Suzuki. Using 1-minute and EBS order-book data for **2018–2020**, Tuesday to Friday only, they find that on gotobi days USDJPY starts rising around 03:00 JST and falls sharply after 9:55 ([arXiv 2301.13204](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.13204v1)). Results net of spreads are below; the spread values used are not stated.

| Rule (gotobi days) | Trades | Profit factor | Win rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1: long 03:00→09:55 JST, only if 25-min SMA crosses above 100-min SMA between 02:30 and 03:00 | 65 | 2.62 | 68% |
| H1 without the SMA filter | 185 | 1.46 | 60% |
| H2: short at 09:55, close 12:00 JST, only if in profit by 09:00 | 113 | 2.09 | 57% |
| H1 + H2 combined | 167 | 2.60 | 63% |

The non-gotobi controls fail. H1 there has a profit factor of about 0.5, and H2 only 1.08–1.20, which supports the idea that the effect comes from settlement flow rather than being a statistical artefact. But the paper has **no out-of-sample test and no year-by-year decay analysis**: rules and data share the same three years ([arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.13204v1)). I found no peer-reviewed or bank estimate for 2021–2026, the period that includes the 2022–2026 yen collapse and wide public awareness of the effect among Japanese retail traders. One broker wrap from 15 June 2026, a gotobi day, reported USD buying *continuing* after the fix ([OANDA Japan](https://www.oanda.jp/lab-education/market_news/mn_1019263_202606151206/)). That is one anecdote, not evidence, but it shows how far a single day can depart from the template.

On balance, the mechanism is the most credible of any effect reviewed here: importer settlement is a real, recurring, non-informational flow. But **assume the effect has decayed toward the 2014–2019 level until your own 2021–2026 test shows otherwise**. The 5-minute version is cost-bound. The hours-long Bessho version captures a larger move per trade, so it has more cost headroom, but it also carries more risk of having been overfitted.

### The London 4pm fix is a volatility event, not an alpha source, for the yen

Before reform, the WM/R fix generated large month-end moves. In Gain Capital data for 2004–2013, about **62% of JPY/USD month-end days** showed pre-fix 1-minute moves beyond the 95th percentile, and post-fix moves were negatively related to pre-fix moves. Yet even then, a JPY/USD reversal strategy had **negative annualised returns after costs at every horizon**: −1.6% at 15 minutes, −0.7% at 5 minutes and −1.2% at 1 minute. JPY/USD was one of only two pairs with no profitable horizon ([Evans, MPRA 81583](https://mpra.ub.uni-muenchen.de/81583/1/MPRA_paper_81583.pdf)). The FCA study found that reversals across the major pairs became insignificant after February 2015, when the fixing window widened to 5 minutes. USDJPY was not in its sample ([FCA OP46](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/occasional-papers/occasional-paper-46.pdf)). Krohn et al. add a yen-specific twist on an hours scale. Unlike other currencies, the yen *depreciates* after the London fix (USDJPY −2.92% annualised post-London in their sign convention, t = −3.03). The yen's European-window reversal portfolio returns about −0.31% a year, so it is not worth trading ([BoC SWP 2021-48](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/swp2021-48.pdf)).

The practical use of the London fix is defensive. The fix is at 16:00 London: **16:00 GMT / 01:00 JST in winter, 15:00 GMT / 00:00 JST during BST**. Keep stops away from it, especially at month-end.

### Month-end equity-hedge rebalancing is a small, testable conditional bet

Melvin and Prins use G10 data for 2004–2012. They find that a 10% month-to-date equity outperformance predicts **about 14bp of currency depreciation from 15:00 to 16:00 London on the last trading day**. The R² is only 0.03, and they report no trading P&L ([EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:finmar:v:22:y:2015:i:c:p:50-72); [ECB draft](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/events/pdf/conferences/131216/Third_FX_Workshop_MELVIN_PRINS_Equity%20hedging%20and%20exchange%20rates%20Nov%202013.pdf)). For the yen the result is murky. Non-bank month-end JPY flow is 1.87 times normal, but the JPY interaction term is insignificant (p = 0.17). That is plausibly because foreign investors hedging Japanese equities and Japanese investors hedging foreign assets push in opposite directions. Hedge ratios also shift by regime: after April 2025 equity funds rapidly raised their USD hedge ratios ([BIS Bulletin 123](https://www.bis.org/publ/bisbull123.pdf)). At 12 observations a year, expect years of data before you know whether it works. Run it as a small side test.

### Intraday seasonality is a "W", and each leg is a few basis points

Breedon and Ranaldo show that currencies tend to depreciate during their home country's trading hours ([EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:wly:jmoncb:v:45:y:2013:i:5:p:953-965)). Krohn et al. refine this into a "W" shape. The USD appreciates into the Tokyo, ECB (14:15 CET) and London fixes and depreciates afterward. Each leg is worth about 2.5bp a day for the dollar portfolio. Most windows lose after full quoted spreads, while at half the spread most are positive, with Sharpe ratios of 0.12–1.03 ([BoC SWP 2021-48](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/swp2021-48.pdf)). This is the same cost bind as the Tokyo fix. These patterns are viable only with passive limit-order execution that earns part of the spread, and IdealPro's exchange-style book does allow resting limits ([IBKB](https://ibkb.interactivebrokers.com/node/1459)). Your fill rate on those limits will decide whether the edge survives.

### Structural flows give a bias, not timing

The March repatriation story is contradicted by the data. Nomura found "no JPY appreciation seasonality in March" ([FXStreet](https://www.fxstreet.com/amp/news/japan-march-seasonality-is-jpy-negative-nomura-201802221007)). Since 1971, March has been USDJPY's third-strongest month ([StoneX](https://www.stonex.com/en-gb/news-and-analysis/forex-seasonality-march-2025-usdjpy-bounce-potential/)). New-NISA household outflows are large but slow-moving. Investment-trust outward investment was **¥11.5trn in 2024**, falling to **¥6.81trn in 2025** ([Mizuho](https://www.mizuhobank.co.jp/forex/pdf/market_analysis/econ250115.pdf); [MOF CY2025](https://www.mof.go.jp/policy/international_policy/reference/balance_of_payments/preliminary/bppicy2025.pdf)). The MOF weekly portfolio-flow release reports the previous week's already-traded flows. Hedge ratios, which determine the FX impact, are not disclosed ([Investing.com](https://investing.com/analysis/short-summary-weekly-mof-portfolio-flows-200197122)). I found **no academic or out-of-sample evidence that the weekly MOF data predicts USDJPY**. Treat it as background reading, not a bot input.

## Trend and carry carry the strongest evidence, and the worst tails

### Volatility-scaled time-series momentum is the most robust systematic signal

Moskowitz, Ooi and Pedersen find significant 12-month time-series momentum in every one of 58 futures and forwards over 1985–2009, currencies included. Returns persist for 1–12 months and partly reverse at longer horizons. Each position is scaled to 40% ex-ante volatility using an EWMA with a 60-day centre of mass. Currency TSMOM has an alpha t-stat of 3.41 ([MOP JFE](https://fairmodel.econ.yale.edu/ec439/jpde.pdf); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2089463)). Trend following has been positive in every decade back to 1880 across 67 markets ([Hurst, Ooi & Pedersen](https://fairmodel.econ.yale.edu/ec439/hurst.pdf)). The counter-evidence matters for design rather than for whether to trade trend at all. Neely, Weller and Ulrich ran true out-of-sample tests and found that published moving-average and filter rules on dollar FX had **lost their profits by the mid-1990s** ([EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:cup:jfinqa:v:44:y:2009:i:02:p:467-488_09)). A 230-year study finds no significant long-run currency momentum ([Chen 2020](https://www.wu.ac.at/fileadmin/wu/d/ri/isk/VSFX_2020/1_2a_Chen_FX_History_20200624.pdf)). So build a multi-lookback, volatility-targeted TSMOM, not a single moving-average crossover.

Also expect a single-pair Sharpe ratio far below the diversified portfolio's figure of above 1. Trend as a style had its second-worst drawdown since 2000 from May 2024 to May 2025: the SG Trend Index fell 20.4% ([Cambridge Associates](https://www.cambridgeassociates.com/en-eu/insight/does-trend-followings-recent-struggle-signal-that-the-strategy-is-structurally-broken/)). Because it trades weekly or monthly, costs are negligible. This is the one strategy here where execution quality barely matters.

### Yen carry pays by the stairs and loses by the elevator

Being short the yen has historically paid. In Brunnermeier, Nagel and Pedersen's sample, short-JPY/long-USD earned about 1.1% a quarter, but with negative skewness. Crash risk rises with the VIX and with crowded speculator positioning ([NBER w14473](https://www.nber.org/papers/w14473.pdf)). Option-hedged G10 carry kept most of its premium over 1990–2012; crash premia explain "at most one-third" ([Jurek, JFE 2014](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:113:y:2014:i:3:p:325-347)). Jurek's earlier working paper, however, found crash-neutral carry indistinguishable from zero over 1999–2008 ([Jurek WP](https://www.johnhcochrane.com/s/jurek_currency.pdf)), so treat the premium as fragile.

August 2024 is the modern template. Yen-funded carry was "the hardest hit", and an estimated ¥40trn of positions was unwound in thin summer markets ([BIS Bulletin 90](https://www.bis.org/publ/bisbull90.pdf)). The yen rose about 14% in under a month ([Advisor Perspectives](https://www.advisorperspectives.com/commentaries/2024/08/13/carry-trade-unwind-over)). In 2026 the policy gap is still about 275bp even after the June BoJ hike to 1.00% ([LSEG](https://www.lseg.com/en/insights/data-analytics/why-rate-differentials-remain-dominant-despite-japans-intervention)). There may have been a further hike in September, and the current BoJ rate should be confirmed against the BoJ itself. Note that a retail account earns the broker's rollover rate, not the policy gap, and IBKR's current USDJPY swap level was not verified for this report.

In this regime, carry and trend are both long USDJPY, so combining them adds little diversification. Both are exposed to the same intervention gaps. What actually helps is a set of **filters** on that one long position:

- **Volatility filter.** Cut exposure when the VIX or USDJPY implied volatility jumps.
- **Positioning filter.** Cut exposure when CFTC speculator net-short-JPY positioning is extreme.
- **Value cap.** Cap the long size while the yen REER sits at its record low (BIS REER 67.73 in January 2026, the lowest since 1973) ([China Daily](https://www.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202602/27/WS69a0d8dfa310d6866eb3a70c.html)).

### CFTC positioning and rate differentials work as regime filters, not triggers

The academic case for COT is about crash risk rather than direction. Larger speculative carry positions predict more negative future skewness ([NBER w14473](https://www.nber.org/papers/w14473.pdf)), and speculators follow 12-month trends ([MOP](https://fairmodel.econ.yale.edu/ec439/jpde.pdf)). The 2026 episodes fit this pattern. Practitioners noted "yen shorts built ahead of yen surge" ([Forex.com](https://www.forex.com/ie/news-and-analysis/fx-futures-positioning-yen-shorts-built-ahead-of-yen-surge-cot-report/)). In the week to 8 September 2026, speculators bought a net 103,000 JPY contracts and flipped net long as the yen rose 4.2% ([Saxo](https://www.home.saxo/en-ch/content/articles/commodities/cot-on-forex-and-commodities---week-to-8-sept-2026-14092026)). No rigorous out-of-sample test of a JPY COT rule exists. The data is as of Tuesday and released on Friday, and extremes can persist for months.

The rate differential was the dominant driver from 2022 to mid-2025. The rolling 12-week correlation with the US–JP 10y spread averaged +0.43 and peaked at 0.91. After October 2025 it **"collapsed to −0.07"**, and USDJPY rose even as the BoJ hiked ([MUFG via FXStreet](https://www.fxstreet.com/news/usd-jpy-decouples-from-yield-spreads-mufg-202512051021); [Daiwa](https://www.dir.co.jp/english/research/report/analysis/20260710_025894.html)). So a spread model should switch itself on and off based on its own rolling correlation. It should not be traded unconditionally.

### Mean-reversion indicators have no USDJPY evidence

I found no peer-reviewed evidence that RSI, Bollinger or short-horizon reversal rules on USDJPY earn money after costs. MOP find continuation, not reversal, at horizons of 1–12 months. Treat any retail backtest of these rules as presumptively data-mined. The only well-documented reversion in USDJPY follows events. The interventions of April and July 2026 both retraced within weeks: "with USD/JPY already trading back near 160, it is hard to make the case that this year's Japanese FX intervention has been effective" ([ING via MENAFN](https://menafn.com/1111184632/USDJPY-Largest-Quarterly-Intervention-Since-2004)). That sample is three campaigns, with large gap risk.

## Event trading is mostly a risk-management problem

**US data.** USDJPY reprices within minutes of NFP and CPI surprises, and deal counts stay elevated for 15–30 minutes ([CIRJE/Hashimoto & Ito](https://www.cirje.e.u-tokyo.ac.jp/research/workshops/macro/macropaper08/macro1127.pdf)). I found no recent, cost-inclusive evidence of exploitable drift. Retail latency and news-time spreads make the first move unreachable.

**BoJ decisions.** The BoJ has "no specific time for the announcement" ([CME/Econoday](https://www.cmegroup.com/education/events/econoday/700126)). The statement usually arrives between the Tokyo lunch break and about **03:30 GMT (12:30 JST)**. The press conference follows at **15:30 JST / 06:30 GMT** ([CME/Econoday](https://www.cmegroup.com/education/events/econoday/663432)). Leaks have pre-empted many Ueda-era decisions, sometimes more than 12 hours ahead ([Japan Times](https://www.japantimes.co.jp/commentary/2023/11/02/japan/boj-media-leaks)), so the real event often happens the evening before, on a headline you will not see in time.

**Intervention.** Japan bought yen at about 145.9 and 151.9 in 2022, 160 and 162 in 2024, 160 in April 2026, and near 164 in July–August 2026. The last of these was a record **¥15.4tn** and the first joint operation with the US since 2011 ([The Edge/Reuters](https://theedgemalaysia.com/node/825595); [Express Tribune/Reuters](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2621796/japan-us-confirm-joint-yen-buying-intervention-signal-more-action)). Initial moves are 4–7+ yen. The October 2022 operation took USDJPY "more than 7 yen" lower ([RTHK/Reuters](https://news.rthk.hk/rthk/en/component/k2/1672210-20221022.htm)). The warning ladder usually escalates in this order:

1. "One-sided and rapid" moves.
2. "Decisive action".
3. Rate checks.

It is not reliable: the 31 July 2026 action came "without advance notice" ([Nation Thailand](https://www.nationthailand.com/news/world/40069264)). As of 13 November 2026, USDJPY was testing 160 for the third time this year, and officials were again warning of "one-sided and rapid" moves ([FXStreet](https://www.fxstreet.com/news/japan-katayama-observing-one-sided-swift-forex-shifts-recently-202611130151)).

For a bot, this translates into hard rules:

- Size every long-USDJPY position above about 158 for a **~5% one-way gap that stops will not contain**.
- Flatten or hedge long positions over Japanese holidays and Friday New York afternoons near the line. The 29 April 2024 and 31 July 2026 interventions fell on exactly those windows ([Reuters via Yahoo](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/currencies/articles/history-japans-intervention-currency-markets-020104940.html)).
- Block new intraday entries in the BoJ window and around top-tier US releases.

**Session breakouts** fail on the evidence available. The only transparent multi-year tests found are for other pairs. One shows a London breakout on EUR/USD as "close to neutral", losing in 2024–2026 ([Backtrex](https://backtrex.com/en/backtests/london-breakout-eur-usd)). Another shows a **−60% total return over ten years on GBP/USD** ([Backtrex](https://backtrex.com/en/backtests/london-breakout-gbp-usd)). USDJPY-specific material is untested marketing, and one popular version actually recommends fading the breakout ([TradingView](https://ar.tradingview.com/chart/USDJPY/1KO8uETw-USDJPY-15M-BIG-BEN-BREAKOUT-STRATEGY-SHORT)). I found no credible evidence for 6J cumulative-delta or volume-profile signals either.

## A ranked shortlist with exact test rules

The ranking weighs four things together: strength of the mechanism, published evidence, cost headroom at IBKR, and how quickly a test reaches statistical power.

On power, take a rough illustration using an assumed standard deviation you should replace with your own measurement. If the 10-minute window around the fix has a standard deviation of about 5bp and the true mean is 1.8bp, then t ≈ 2 needs about (2 × 5 / 1.8)² ≈ 31 observations. Gotobi days alone give about 70 a year. So the Tokyo-fix test can deliver a verdict on 2021–2026 data. A monthly trend rule on one pair, by contrast, needs decades of data to separate a Sharpe of 0.3 from zero, so judge it on the broad literature plus robustness checks rather than on its own backtest.

Rule definitions used in the table:

- **Gotobi days:** the 5th, 10th, 15th, 20th, 25th and 30th, plus the last business day of the month. A date that falls on a Japanese holiday or weekend moves to the previous business day.
- **Clocks:** JST has no DST. US and UK times shift; both conversions are shown where they apply.

| Rank | Strategy | Test rule (GMT / JST) | Evidence and cost check |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1a | Gotobi pre-fix long | Gotobi days, Tue–Fri. Buy 23:00 GMT (prev. day) / 08:00 JST. Exit 00:50–00:55 GMT / 09:50–09:55 JST. Variant: Bessho entry at 18:00 GMT / 03:00 JST with the 25/100-min SMA cross filter (02:30–03:00 JST). Control: same rule on non-gotobi days. | Ito-Yamada; Bessho in-sample PF 1.46–2.62. Use resting limits. Require net > 0 at your *measured* spread for 2021–2026, and stability year by year. |
| 1b | Post-Tokyo-fix short | Sell 00:55–01:00 GMT / 09:55–10:00 JST. Cover 01:30–03:00 GMT / 10:30–12:00 JST. Filter: only if USDJPY is up since 00:00 GMT / 09:00 JST. Test all days and gotobi days separately. | Krohn: largest yen reversal but decayed to about 2% a year (2014–19); −6.5% a year at full spread. |
| 2 | Vol-scaled TSMOM + carry | Daily close (e.g. 22:00 GMT / 07:00 JST, after the NY rollover; 21:00 GMT / 06:00 JST in US summer). Signal = average sign of 1-, 3- and 12-month excess return (spot + carry). Target 10% annualised vol with EWMA (60-day COM). Rebalance weekly. | MOP, Hurst et al. Costs negligible. Expect a single-pair Sharpe well under 1, with intervention gaps. |
| 3 | Crash and intervention overlay on rank 2 | Halve the long size if: VIX or 1m USDJPY implied vol is in the top decile of its 1-year range; CFTC TFF leveraged-fund net-short-JPY is above the 90th percentile over 3 years (updated Fri, data as of Tue); spot is above 158; or the warning ladder hits "decisive action" / a rate check. Flat over JP holidays and Friday NY close when above 158. | BNP crowding and VIX; intervention record. Judge by reduced drawdown, not by return. |
| 4 | Month-end equity-hedge flow | Last business day. Sign = MTD TOPIX-in-USD minus S&P 500 return, trade only when \|diff\| > 3%. Japan outperforming → long USDJPY; US outperforming → short. Trade 15:00→16:00 London: winter 15:00–16:00 GMT / 00:00–01:00 JST; BST 14:00–15:00 GMT / 23:00–24:00 JST. | Melvin-Prins ~14bp per 10% outperformance; JPY sign ambiguous; 12 trades a year. Small size only. |
| 5 | Rate-differential regime switch | Weekly. Go with the 4-week change in the US–JP 2y spread *only* while the 12-week rolling correlation is above +0.4. Otherwise flat. Data: FRED DGS2, MOF JGB CSV. | Worked 2022–mid 2025; correlation −0.07 after Oct 2025. Use mainly as a filter for rank 2. |

Deprioritise the following until the top five are done: London-fix fades, Asian-range or London-open breakouts, RSI and Bollinger reversion, NFP/CPI/BoJ scalping, intervention-fade entries, 6J order flow, MOF weekly flows and calendar-month seasonality. Each either has negative or absent cost-inclusive evidence, or a sample too small to test.

On implementation, five practical points:

- **Test data.** Use tick or 1-minute data that includes bid and ask (Dukascopy and TrueFX are common free sources; their coverage was not verified for this report).
- **Live spreads.** Before trusting any backtest, log IBKR's live USD.JPY top-of-book for a few weeks at 00:45–01:05 GMT, around the London fix and at 21:00–22:00 GMT.
- **Order routing.** Send IdealPro orders of at least $100k so the $2 minimum stops dominating the commission. Use limits for the fix trades.
- **Futures.** Use 6J only if you want centralised data. MJY's 1.4bp tick is too coarse for fix-window trades.
- **API library.** ib_insync is no longer maintained, and the community fork ib_async is the usual Python route. This is from background knowledge, not checked against primary pages here.

## Conclusion

For USDJPY, the evidence suggests the reliable edges come from *who has to trade*: importers at 9:55 JST, hedgers at month-end, carry holders who must unwind, and a Ministry of Finance that intervenes near 160. Each of these is a real flow, but the time-of-day effects have been arbitraged toward the cost floor, and the macro flows show up mainly as tail risk rather than drift. That shapes what to build. The first deliverable should be a measurement harness: spreads, fill rates on resting limits, and a gotobi-versus-control event study on 2021–2026 data. A trading bot comes after that. The trend and carry core should be judged on how well its crash filters protect it, since over the next year its returns will depend mostly on whether it is positioned correctly when the next intervention or unwind hits.

The main open questions are empirical and are things you can answer yourself, not gaps in the literature. Has the Tokyo-fix effect survived the 2022–2026 yen move and its own popularity? What spread does IBKR actually quote at 00:55 GMT? Does a passive limit strategy get filled when the flow is genuinely one-sided? A clean test of the first question on your own data would beat any published figure here, because nobody has published it.
